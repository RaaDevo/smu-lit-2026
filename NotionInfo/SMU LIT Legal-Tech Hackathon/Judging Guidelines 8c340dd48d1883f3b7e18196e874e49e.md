# Judging Guidelines

This document explains the score descriptors to be used.

**1. Criteria and Score Descriptors**

**1.1 Relevance to Problem Statement (30%)**

Does the solution address the problem statement the team selected, and does it solve a real, well-understood pain point in that area of legal practice?

| **Score** | **1 - 2 (Weak)** | **3 - 5 (Adequate)** | **6 – 8 (Good)** | **9 – 10 (Excellent)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descriptor** | Solution is loosely connected to the problem statement or misunderstands the underlying legal issue. | Solution addresses the problem statement but only partially, or targets a minor aspect of it. | Solution clearly addresses the problem statement and demonstrates a sound understanding of the legal issue. | Solution directly and fully addresses the problem statement, with clear evidence the team understands the practical legal pain point and who it affects. |

**1.2 Technical Feasibility (25%)**

Does the solution work, and is it built in a way that could realistically be developed further? Includes code quality, use of AI tools, and whether the team can explain their own build.

| **Score** | **1 - 2 (Weak)** | **3 – 5 (Adequate)** | **6 – 8 (Good)** | **9 – 10 (Excellent)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descriptor** | Solution does not work, is a mock-up only, or the team cannot explain how their own code functions. | Solution partially works or relies heavily on hard-coded/scripted demo scenarios; team has limited grasp of implementation details. | Solution works as demonstrated with only minor gaps; team can explain their build, including which AI tools were used and how. | Solution is fully functional, technically sound, and reasonably robust; team demonstrates clear command of their own architecture and design decisions, including AI tool usage and disclosure. |

**1.3 Innovation (25%)**

Does the solution offer a genuinely new approach, or a meaningfully better way of solving an existing problem, within the legal-tech space?

| **Score** | **1 - 2 (Weak)** | **3 - 5 (Adequate)** | **6 – 8 (Good)** | **9 – 10 (Excellent)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descriptor** | Solution closely replicates an existing tool or approach with no meaningful differentiation. | Solution has some original elements but is largely incremental or a straightforward combination of existing tools. | Solution offers a clear, original angle on the problem, or applies technology in a way not commonly seen in this space. | Solution is highly original, offering a novel approach or unlocking a use case that existing tools do not address well. |

**1.4 Presentation Quality (20%)**

Was the pitch clear, well-structured, and convincing within the 4-minute time limit, and did the team handle questions well?

| **Score** | **1 - 2 (Weak)** | **3 – 5 (Adequate)** | **6 – 8 (Good)** | **9 – 10 (Excellent)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Descriptor** | Pitch is unclear or disorganised, significantly over/under time, or team struggles to answer basic questions. | Pitch covers the essentials but lacks polish, pacing issues, or answers to questions are only partially satisfactory. | Pitch is clear, well-paced within the time limit, and team answers questions competently. | Pitch is clear, engaging, and well within time; team handles questions confidently and persuasively, including on legal/ethical implications. |

**3. Note**

a. Ties will be broken using the following order:

1. Relevance to Problem Statement
2. Technical Feasibility
3. Innovation
4. Presentation Quality
